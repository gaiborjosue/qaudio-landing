# Q Audio

A monochrome hi-fi storefront prototype for Q Audio in Cambridge, Massachusetts. The landing experience, category exploration, brand destinations, visit section with live store hours, contact footer, the news feed with its migrated posts, the QDeal page with its Supabase-managed deals, and the shop’s category/brand product listings with expandable product details are implemented. The owner manages shop products in the admin’s Products tab and categories and brands in Metadata, and store hours, news posts, QDeal deals, and the shop’s Instagram posts by chatting with an AI site assistant, both at `/admin` (see [Admin assistant](#admin-assistant)). The shop catalog currently holds one sample product; checkout, cart, and backend message delivery are not implemented.

## Setup and commands

Run `pnpm install`, copy [.env.example](.env.example) to `.env.local` (git-ignored) and fill in the keys, then run `pnpm dev` and open [localhost:3000](http://localhost:3000). News, QDeal deals, the shop catalog, and the home page’s store hours come from Supabase, so `pnpm dev` and `pnpm build` need `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` and a reachable project; `next.config.ts` stops with an error without the URL (see [Supabase](#supabase)). The admin assistant also needs `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, plus `AI_GATEWAY_API_KEY` for automatic chat model selection (routing failures fall back to Terra), and optionally `SUPERMEMORY_API_KEY` for its memory, and `ZERNIO_API_KEY` with `ZERNIO_PROFILE_ID` for Instagram (see [Instagram via Zernio](#instagram-via-zernio)). `pnpm dev` starts it alongside the site. A pnpm lockfile is included. `package.json` requires Node 24 or later (`engines`), which eve needs; `@supabase/supabase-js` needs Node 22 or later. Epilogue and Bodoni Moda load through `next/font/google`.

Declared versions include Next.js **16.4.2**, React/React DOM **19.3.1**, TypeScript **^5**, Tailwind CSS **^4**, GSAP **^3.15.0**, `@gsap/react` **^2.1.2**, Motion **^14.7.1**, shadcn **^4.22.1**, `@supabase/supabase-js` **2.118.0** and `@supabase/ssr` **0.12.7** (both pinned), eve **1.0.0-rc.1** (pinned; a release candidate), AI SDK `ai` **^7.0.159**, `@openrouter/ai-sdk-provider` **^3.1.0**, Zod **^4.6.7**, Streamdown **^2.7.1**, `react-day-picker` **^10.0.2** (for shadcn’s Calendar), `@shadcn/react` **^0.3.1** (for shadcn’s Message Scroller), `@zernio/node` **0.2.1459** (pinned), and sharp **0.35.5** (pinned; Instagram photo conversion in the agent, and Prefill with Tune’s photo staging). No Tavily SDK is installed; Prefill calls Tavily’s REST API directly. See [package.json](package.json) for all dependencies and version ranges.

Voice dictation in the admin chat also needs the server-only `GROQ_API_KEY` (see [Voice input](#voice-input)). Add it to `.env.local` locally and the Next.js service’s environment in production. The Products tab’s Prefill with Tune needs the server-only `TAVILY_API_KEY` the same way (see [Prefill with Tune](#prefill-with-tune)); without it, the rest of the form works and Prefill says it isn’t set up.

## Experience and routes

Every full page load starts with a liquid glass loader (`components/site-loader.tsx`), built on the ReactDrops “Liquid Glass Logo” component (`components/liquid-glass-logo*.{tsx,ts,css}`). The full-screen white overlay renders it at `height: 480` (capped at `100svh`), with `ending="logo"`, `theme="light"` for the light-page optics, `logoColor="original"` so the logo keeps its own black, and `background="#ffffff"`. The logo is `public/loader-logo.svg`. Each pass keeps the component’s look:
- A droplet becomes a sphere, then a rounded glass tile.
- Its wavelength-based bevel refraction bends the logo, with chromatic edge streaks.
- The waves settle, then the glass clears to leave the original logo alone at its normal size, with no wordmark reveal.

The component’s measured responsive fitting sizes the glass to the screen, so it shrinks on phones. While the page loads, `loop` replays the 1.8s entrance after a 1.6s hold on the logo. After hydration, `window.load` and `document.fonts.ready`, looping turns off, so the current pass lands on the logo; a first visit always sees one full entrance. The loader then holds 450ms and fades out over 600ms with a slight scale. It knows the logo has landed through a small local addition to the component: `time()` on its handle, compared with the exported `liquidGlassLogoFinishTime`. A 6s limit covers a renderer that never finishes. When it’s done, the WebGL view unmounts to free the graphics context, and the overlay stays as `data-state="done"`, its state attribute moving `loading` → `finishing` → `leaving` → `done`. The markup is server-rendered, so the white overlay covers the page before hydration. Page scrolling is locked during loading and finishing, and deep links such as `/#visit` still land correctly. Reduced motion shows the finished logo straight away and leaves once the page is ready. Without WebGL2, the component’s image fallback shows the plain logo. A `<noscript>` style hides the loader without JavaScript, and a 12s CSS failsafe hides it if the script never runs. The CSS transition in `globals.css` (`.site-loader`) and `FADE_MS` in the loader must change together.

- `/`: header with the logo at the top left and a fixed main menu at the top right, dithered hero over a faded showroom photo with a today’s-hours glance pill and a “Contact us” link to the footer form, pinned listening story that continues into a horizontal Q Audio history, category explorer at `#categories`, brand wall at `#product-lines`, visit section at `#visit`, and contact footer at `#contact`.
- `/shop/[category]`: the category icon and title, then that category’s published products as cards that expand into product details (see [Shop](#shop)). Static params come from the `catalog_categories` table; a category added there later renders on its first visit. The category comes from the URL, so it streams inside `<Suspense>` from the cache while the shared shell (header and menu) prefetches. The category artwork loads with `loading="eager"`, since it is the above-the-fold LCP image (a lazy one triggers Next’s dev LCP warning).
- `/shop`: every published product under “The collection”. `/shop?brand=<slug>` filters to one brand from `catalog_brands` and shows its logo, its name as the title, and a removable “Brand: …” chip; clearing it returns to `/shop`. Unknown brand values return not found. The brand logo also loads eagerly for the same LCP reason.
- `/used`: a permanent (308) redirect to `/shop/used-equipment`, set in `next.config.ts`, so the live qaudio.com address keeps working.
- `/products/[slug]`: one product as its own page, at the live qaudio.com product address. It shows a “← category” link, the same details as the dialog (with the name as the page’s `h1` and no close button), and schema.org `Product` data (JSON-LD) for search results. Its metadata sets the title, description, and Open Graph image. Published products are prerendered from `generateStaticParams`; newer ones render on first visit. Opened from a shop list instead, the same URL shows the details dialog over the list (see [Shop](#shop)).
- Unknown addresses (such as `/nope`) and any `notFound()` call show `app/not-found.tsx` (“Page not found”, with the logo header and menu). Unknown addresses return HTTP 404. Unknown category, product, and news slugs and unknown `?brand=` values return HTTP 200 with a `noindex` robots tag and the “Page not found” title instead: those pages stream their URL-dependent part inside `<Suspense>`, and Next can’t change the status once streaming has started. Uncaught errors below the root layout show `app/error.tsx` (“Something went wrong”, with Try again, using Next 16’s `retry()`, and Back to home); errors in the root layout itself fall back to `app/global-error.tsx`. They share `components/status-page.tsx`.
- `/news`: one-column news feed, newest first, with progressive loading (details below).
- `/news/[slug]`: article pages at the live qaudio.com permalinks. Published posts are prerendered from `generateStaticParams`; posts published later render on first visit. Unknown or draft slugs return not found.
- `/api/news?cursor=&limit=`: JSON feed pages (`{ items, nextCursor }`). Omit `cursor` for the newest cards. `limit` defaults to 3 and allows up to 24. A malformed cursor or limit returns 400.
- `POST /api/admin/speech`: admin-only, same-origin audio upload; returns English text for the chat draft through Groq Whisper Large v3. See [Voice input](#voice-input).
- `POST /api/admin/product-page/check` and `POST /api/admin/product-page/prefill`: admin-only, same-origin JSON; the product form’s manufacturer link check and Prefill with Tune. See [Prefill with Tune](#prefill-with-tune).
- `/qdeal`: live deals from Supabase (currently the Sonos closeouts), then Q Audio’s “Proper advice is priceless!” text (details below).
- `/admin`: the owner’s workspace (signed-in admins only): the chat with the site assistant, and the Products tab at `/admin/products`. `/admin/s/<session>` reopens a conversation, `/admin/login` signs in (with a password reset form), and `/admin/set-password` follows a reset link. `/auth/confirm` receives Supabase’s email links, and `/eve/v1/*` is the agent’s API. See [Admin assistant](#admin-assistant).

The home page’s main menu (`components/site-nav.tsx`) is the React Bits PillNav, at size `lg` with the light theme, without its logo since the header already shows ours. It lists Home, News (`/news`), QDeal (`/qdeal`) and Used Equipment, which links straight to the `/shop/used-equipment` category page (the live site’s `/used` address redirects there), so that item is active on that page. The current page comes from `usePathname()` and gets PillNav’s active dot and `aria-current`; pages below a section (`/news/<post>`) keep that section active. That read sits inside `<Suspense>`: while Next prerenders a shared shell whose path isn’t known yet (dynamic and intercepted routes), the menu renders without an active item, and the highlighted one streams in. Links use Next’s `Link`, and hovering an item rolls in the dark pill fill. The bar uses PillNav’s glass surface with extra frost, set through its CSS variables: a 30px blur (from 20px), saturation lowered to 1.2 so colorful sections behind stay neutral, and a 55% white body. It’s fixed at the top right (`z-index: 50`, under the loader), vertically centered on the logo with the header’s side inset (48px, 30px at 1000px and below, 22px at 600px and below), so it stays in view while scrolling. It sits outside `ScrollExperience`, because the pinned stage must not contain it, and comes right after the skip link in tab order. The fixed `.site-nav` wrapper spans the header beside the logo so PillNav can measure when the items no longer fit and collapse into its menu button with a frosted dropdown (as on phones). The wrapper ignores pointer events; only the pill, button and dropdown take them. The news pages share it through `app/news/layout.tsx`, `/qdeal` renders the same header and footer shell in its page, and the shop and product pages share the header and menu (no footer) through `components/site-header.tsx`, used by `app/shop/layout.tsx`, `app/products/layout.tsx`, and the 404 and error pages.

The root `<html>` carries `data-scroll-behavior="smooth"`. Since Next 16, Next no longer switches off the global `scroll-behavior: smooth` during client-side route changes unless that attribute is set. Without it, its scroll-to-top on navigation animated, then corrected to the page segment, so a news article opened from far down the feed landed 148px down with the header hidden.

The GSAP/ScrollTrigger story pins the stage while the hero exits and vinyl travels through opening text rows. The record tilts, grows, lands on the turntable, and the tonearm drops onto it.

The same pin then plays Q Audio’s history sideways (`components/story-history.tsx`):
- **Transition:** after a short hold on the finished quote, the quote slides off to the left as a horizontal track of seven slides arrives from the right.
- **Chapters:** A1 1973 (the classifieds), A2 late ’70s (tube exports to collectors in Japan, Asia and Europe, from the original Tech HiFi store, on a world map), A3 1980 (95 Vassar Street becomes a store), A4 1982 (the first CD players, the *New York Times* ads, Krell and Pyramid), A5 1982–2013 (the world’s smallest hi-fi store, with the old showroom photo), B1 2013 (the move to 5 Brookline Street) and B2 Today (a 2×2 contact sheet of shop photos). The numbering reads like a record: side A is the past, side B the present.
- **Layout:** chapters without media show their year large. Each slide sits between the header and the turntable, using the same formula as `.record-player` (bottom 5%, 10:3 aspect; 8% and 94vw on phones). On wide screens the media is beside the text; at 1000px and below it’s stacked above it, and year and map slides are vertically centered.
- **World map (A2):** the Aceternity World Map (`components/ui/world-map.tsx`, wrapped by `components/history-map.tsx`) draws black routes from Cambridge to Tokyo, Hong Kong and Frankfurt, standing in for Japan, Asia and Europe, over a dotted world. The wrapper watches its slide’s `data-active` attribute, so the routes draw in sequence (0.5s apart) and the endpoints pulse each time the slide becomes active. They’re hidden again once you scroll past, ready to redraw. Reduced motion shows them already drawn, without pulsing. The map is decorative (`aria-hidden`); the slide text tells the story. It’s about 640px wide at its 2:1 shape on wide screens, and full width when stacked.
- **Spacing:** the gap between slides is wider than the side inset, so the next slide peeks in from the right but the previous one fully leaves.
- **Photos:** grayscale except on the active slide, which turns color once it’s more than halfway in (`data-active`, set from the timeline time). Photos have Apple-style continuous squircle corners (`corner-shape: squircle`, behind `@supports`): 32px on single photos and 22px on contact-sheet tiles (24px and 16px on phones). Browsers without `corner-shape` get plain rounded corners of 14px and 10px instead, since a squircle corner reads tighter than a round one at the same radius.
- **Pacing:** each slide is one eased step of the timeline (`SLIDE_DURATION` 1, about 0.55 screen heights of scrolling). Inside the history range, ScrollTrigger snaps to whole slides with `inertia: false`, so a fast flick can’t skip the story; elsewhere scrolling is free.
- **Tonearm:** it doubles as the progress bar. As the slides play, the headshell (`.turntable-headshell`) slides 90 SVG units inward across the record and the three `.turntable-tube` paths stretch to meet it. After a short hold on the last slide, the arm lifts back to rest and the pin releases into the categories.
- **Length:** the pin now runs about 15 timeline units (≈8.3 screen heights, about 7.4k px at 900px tall). Deep links such as `/#visit` still land correctly.
- **Reduced motion and no script:** no pin; the history is a plain vertical list after the listening story.
- **Skip Story:** a small glass link with a down arrow appears when the first history chapter becomes active, remaining hidden during the opening phrase slide. It sits to the left of the vinyl (below the turntable on narrower screens) and jumps instantly to the categories and focuses their heading, finishing any pending snap/scrub work first. Its faint monochrome aurora is a local CSS treatment inspired by [React Bits Aurora](https://reactbits.dev/backgrounds/aurora), with no added dependency; reduced motion keeps it static, and reduced transparency hides the effect. Without the pin, the link sits before the history list and still targets the categories.
- **Copy:** the slide text is drawn from Q Audio’s published history, but the owner should confirm it, especially the dates and the “very first CD players” line.

Responsive layouts, reduced-motion fallbacks, and section-anchor restoration after pinned layout setup are implemented. Back, forward, and reload also return to the exact scroll position, including mid-story. Browsers restore their offset before the pin spacer exists (now ≈7.4k px of extra height at 900px tall), so they used to land early, inside the record-player story. [lib/scroll-memory.ts](lib/scroll-memory.ts) therefore saves `scrollY` per history entry in `sessionStorage`, keyed by an id added to `history.state`. After fonts, `load`, and `ScrollTrigger.refresh()`, the scroll experience re-applies it with an instant scroll. A saved position takes precedence over a `#hash`, while a fresh visit or hash link has no saved entry and behaves as before. Saving starts only after that restore, so transient positions during load can't overwrite it. Only history states carrying Next’s `__NA` marker are ever extended, because Next reloads the page when it traverses to a state without it. The brand wall’s plain `<a>` tiles make full page loads, which is where the bug showed. The reduced-motion path has no pin and relies on native restoration. For Safari parity, `.vinyl-surround`/`.vinyl-anchor` use an explicit `height` rather than `aspect-ratio`. WebKit stretched the padded aspect-ratio box when its children used percentage heights, which made the disc an ellipse and shifted every scroll position. The tilt’s 1200px perspective also comes from CSS `perspective` on `.is-scroll-active .vinyl-surround` instead of GSAP `transformPerspective`, because WebKit drew the in-transform perspective less foreshortened, so the record never lay flat on the platter. On phones (≤600px), the story text uses `7.1vw`, weight 500, −0.03em tracking, 1.2 line height, and a word-space gap between row halves. Each row parts at its own word break, and the travelling record (about 17% of the width) shifts sideways to that break. That lets the longer halves use the full width before the record moves to the platter as usual.

The category See Products link, visit Directions link, and footer Send Message button share the local LiquidGlass component and `.glass-action` styling for the Aceternity-style treatment: `radius=31`, `scale=120`, `aberration=56`, `bevelDepth=60`, `frost=0`, `tint=0`, plus a mouse-position highlight. Their focus ring appears only for keyboard focus (`:has(:focus-visible)`), not after mouse clicks. SVG backdrop refraction targets Chromium. Reduced-transparency preferences select an opaque background with no backdrop filter. Safari-like user agents or a failed CSS URL backdrop-filter support check select the simple blur/saturation fallback (blur follows `frost`, currently `0`). The optional `redOffset` and `greenOffset` (default 0) push only that channel further.

Under the brand wall’s title sits a search (`components/ui/search.tsx`, Bencho’s Search, styled by the “Search” block at the end of `app/globals.css`): a circle flush with the title’s left edge that opens into a pill to the right, with its left edge and lens staying put (on phones, where the title is centered, it is centered under it and grows from its middle, as Bencho drew it). The left-pinning is CSS only: the frame slides by half the field’s growth, following the spring. It filters the wall as you type, matching brand names only, ignoring case, accents, spaces and punctuation (`pro ject` finds Pro-Ject, `kl` finds Klipsch and KLH). While a search is active the drift stops:
- **Few results:** the wall stops and the matching tiles settle in the middle of it (`still` on DriftWall), so `sonos` leaves just Sonos. A short fade covers the swap, and the tiles stay links to `/shop?brand=<slug>`.
- **No results:** a quiet “No brands found”. A polite live region announces the count (“1 brand shown”) for screen readers.
- **Many results:** `still` is only a request. DriftWall holds still only while every row fits the wall (up to 15 results at 1440×900, 9 on a phone), and otherwise keeps drifting, so no result is ever clipped out of reach.
- **Clearing:** Escape, or emptying the field, restores the full drifting wall. Whitespace alone doesn’t count as a search.
- **Reduced motion:** the existing static grid filters in place. Its height is held for the duration of a search, with the results centered level with the field, so the field doesn’t move under the visitor’s typing.

Hours live in Supabase (`store_weekly_hours` and `store_hours_changes`; see [Supabase](#supabase)) and are read by [lib/store-hours-data.ts](lib/store-hours-data.ts) (server-only). The home page fetches them on the server and passes them to the hero pill and the visit section as a `StoreSchedule`. Reads are cached under the `store-hours` tag with the `content` cache profile (see [Caching](#caching)), so edits appear within 5 minutes. Edit them by chatting at `/admin` (see [Admin assistant](#admin-assistant)); after an approved save, the admin chat calls `updateTag("store-hours")`, so the site updates right away. They can also be edited in the Supabase table editor:
- Each weekday row (`weekday` 0 = Monday) has `kind` set to `open` (with `opens_at`/`closes_at`), `appointment`, or `closed`.
- For a special day or closure, add a row to `store_hours_changes` with `starts_on`, an inclusive `ends_on` (the same date for one day), `kind`, any times, and an optional `note`.

Changes disappear automatically after their last date. The pure status and formatting helpers in [lib/store-hours.ts](lib/store-hours.ts) take the schedule as an argument. Time-dependent details (today pill, status, notices, dated overrides) render on the client after hydration and refresh each minute. The server-rendered HTML shows only the regular week, and the section sits far enough below the fold that the update is not seen. The phone line (“Phones are answered daily, 10 am – 7 pm”) is still `PHONE_HOURS` in code.

### Voice input

The microphone immediately left of Send records a message for review. Clicking it requests browser microphone permission when needed; nothing records on page load. During recording it becomes Stop, and a dense monochrome Web Audio waveform and elapsed time replace the message field. Tiny 2px bars capture microphone loudness every 50ms: new speech enters from the right and the recorded history scrolls smoothly left, with a fine baseline during pauses. Stop releases the microphone immediately and freezes the bars while Groq processes the recording. The resulting English text is appended to any existing draft, the field regains focus, and the owner can edit and send it; dictation never sends a chat message itself. Attached photos stay in place. Send is disabled until dictation finishes.

Groq’s [speech-to-text API](https://console.groq.com/docs/speech-to-text) runs `whisper-large-v3` through `/audio/translations`, which detects the input language and returns English, including for Polish speech. `/api/admin/speech` checks the signed-in admin and request origin on every upload, fixes the model and translation options on the server, and returns only text or a safe error. `GROQ_API_KEY` stays in the Next.js server environment, never the browser or agent. Audio is sent to Groq after Stop; the app does not save recordings in Storage, chat history, or memory. The reviewed text becomes an ordinary chat message only when sent.

Recordings stop automatically at three minutes and are limited to 4 MB (including a separate bounded multipart-body limit to stay below the host’s request cap). MediaRecorder selects WebM/Opus, MP4, or Ogg/Opus according to browser support. HTTPS or localhost is required; plain HTTP on a LAN hostname cannot request microphone access. Escape or the cancel icon discards dictation; switching to Products, starting New chat, hiding the browser tab, or leaving the page cancels capture, pending permission results, and transcription. A late permission grant has its tracks stopped, and late transcripts cannot populate a different chat. Permission, device, connection, and transcription errors preserve the draft and photos. Reduced motion holds the waveform still; animations, audio contexts, tracks, timers, and requests clean up when hidden or unmounted.

The waveform in [components/admin/voice-input.tsx](components/admin/voice-input.tsx) is an original compact implementation inspired by ElevenLabs’ [Bar Visualizer](https://ui.elevenlabs.io/docs/components/bar-visualizer), with no ElevenLabs dependency or service. [lib/admin/voice-recorder.ts](lib/admin/voice-recorder.ts) owns the cancellable recording lifecycle; [lib/admin/speech-api.ts](lib/admin/speech-api.ts) owns request validation and Groq translation. `pnpm test:speech` covers both with mocked APIs. Browser microphone and visual checks remain user-led.

Returning to the open Connections popup checks again; clicking a connected account checks manually. Connect and Reconnect are shown in that same popup when needed. No Meta app ID, secret, configuration ID, redirect URI, local token encryption key, or application OAuth callback is needed.
- **Status:** `/api/instagram/status` verifies the signed-in admin on every request, then lists Instagram accounts for the configured profile using [lib/admin/zernio.ts](lib/admin/zernio.ts) and its tested API helper. Requests are uncached, use a ten-second timeout, and reject redirects. The helper also checks each returned account's profile/platform, excludes hidden, inactive, and reconnect-required accounts, and returns only IDs, usernames, and safe status messages. It never forwards provider bodies or errors. Multiple connected accounts each get their own icon, username, and green-check row, with the platform and connected state in the accessible label. Status comes from Zernio's current account record; it does not prove publishing permissions. The header checks status on mount, window focus, browser visibility, and popup opening, limited to once per thirty seconds; explicit account clicks check immediately. One shared summary drives the icon badge and popup rows, so the badge works before opening the list. A connected response with other in-scope accounts inactive, missing a username, or needing reconnection includes only a safe `needsAttention` flag and message: healthy rows remain usable, while the header warns and the popup offers Manage connection. Hidden and unrelated accounts do not affect the summary. Effects release requests, observers, and listeners when the workspace unmounts or React Activity hides it. The popup respects reduced motion.
- **Storage and security:** the SDK and API key stay on the server, behind admin authorization. There are no social tokens in Supabase, browser state, Tune, or agent memory. The cleanup migration `20261104022741_replace_meta_oauth_with_zernio.sql` drops the former Meta connection and state tables; the original migration remains as history. No new tables or RLS policies are needed: Zernio holds the posts. Provider failures clear the green check and return a safe retry or configuration message. The agent process reads `ZERNIO_API_KEY` and `ZERNIO_PROFILE_ID` too (`eve dev` loads `.env.local`; set them on the agent's service in deployment), so the publishing tools can call Zernio.
- **Publishing with Tune:** the `instagram` skill and five deferred `instagram__` tools let the owner draft, schedule, post, change, retry, and delete photo posts, carousels (2–10 photos), and stories, including posts made from a news post, QDeal, or product. Tune writes the caption to the skill's rules (opening line before Instagram's "more", no clickable links, a few hashtags).
  - **Tools:** `get_account` (connection, posting health, and what's left of Instagram's publishing limit) and `list_posts` (status, store-time dates, caption, Instagram link, failure reason) are reads; `save_post`, `delete_post`, and `retry_post` need approval. Everything goes through [lib/admin/instagram-posts.ts](lib/admin/instagram-posts.ts), which parses each reply down to an allowlist, rewords errors (no provider payloads, keys, links, or token-like strings), and checks that a post targets only this profile's connected Instagram accounts before changing it. Tools check the signed-in admin themselves ([agent/lib/instagram.ts](agent/lib/instagram.ts)), because Zernio calls don't go through RLS.
  - **Photos:** a post takes photos from the media library: attached `uploads/` photos (copied into `instagram/` on save, like news), or a product's or news post's saved photos. The agent converts each with sharp: upright, fitted to the post's frame, sRGB JPEG at 1080px wide, metadata stripped. Feed posts keep the first photo's shape within Instagram's 4:5 to 1.91:1 (a carousel's other photos take the same frame); stories are 9:16, black bars unless the photo is already tall. `fit: "contain"` keeps whole photos with bars. It refuses a photo whose real shape differs from the stated size, so the preview's crop holds. Each JPEG goes up through Zernio's presigned upload, and the post is created right away, inside Zernio's 7-day window for uploads.
  - **Timing and safety:** `when` is a Zernio draft, now, or a store time from a few minutes to 7 days ahead (checked when proposed and again when approved). Each call sends an idempotency key derived from the session and call id, so a replayed call returns the original post, and a Zernio duplicate (same content to the account within 24 hours) returns the existing post instead of a second one. Tune stores its own record (kind, fit, photos) in the post's `metadata.tune`; only drafts and scheduled posts with that record can be changed, and an unchanged photo set reuses its uploads. Published posts can't be deleted through Zernio; the owner removes them in the Instagram app.
  - **Preview:** [instagram-preview.tsx](components/admin/instagram-preview.tsx) shows the post as Instagram will, centered on a light grey stage in the approval card. It uses the same frame and crop as the conversion, photos in color (an intentional exception to the grayscale rule), and Instagram's system font. A feed post has the header (the shop's logo as the profile picture, the username, and a "…"), Instagram's mock actions (like, comment, share, save), the caption folded after 125 characters with "more", hashtags and @mentions in Instagram's link blue, and the post date ("Just now" for now; none for a draft). Like is the only working action: a toggle, which a double-click on the photo also sets with Instagram's heart burst (none under reduced motion). A carousel swipes like Instagram's: touch and trackpads scroll natively, a mouse drags it (a swipe of 15% of a photo turns the page), the arrow keys step it when focused, and its arrows step it too, with a 1/N count and blue dots between the actions. While a carousel waits for approval, a row of its photos sits above the post: thumbnails that fit the post's width, each with its position number on a dark pill over a light scrim, and a ring on the one showing. Drag a thumbnail to a new place (Motion's `Reorder`, horizontal; or focus it and press the left and right arrow keys, announced) and the post follows at once, scrolling to the moved photo; click one to show it. A new first photo can change the post's shape, as it would on Instagram. Once the order differs, a **Reset order** joins Approve and Deny. Approving (whichever way it goes out) sends the new order in the `Approved as:` line's `photos`, so the re-proposal the chat approves has exactly those photos in that order ([lib/admin/approval-choice.ts](lib/admin/approval-choice.ts)); a denial note carries it in a hidden `Photo order:` line, so the next proposal keeps it. A story is a 9:16 screen with its progress bar, header, and "Send message" reply row over the photo.
- **Not wired yet:** videos and Reels (the composer uploads photos only), comments, DMs, insights and best times, webhooks (post status is read on request), Zernio's queue slots, and generating new images. Captions come from Tune's copy rules in the skill; richer content generation can build on it later.
- **Validation:** `pnpm test:zernio` exercises the real SDK with mocked transport: scoped bearer requests, payload allowlisting, profile/platform isolation, inactive/revoked accounts, malformed replies, rate limits, timeouts, and error redaction; for publishing, the post body, idempotency key, duplicates, foreign and published posts, presigned uploads, account health, the 7-day limit, Instagram's input rules, a real sharp conversion, and which re-proposals an Approve choice (how a post, news post, or deal goes out, and a carousel's arranged order) approves. `node scripts/verify-agent-catalog.mjs` covers the `instagram__` group's discovery, approvals, and previews. Also run lint, typecheck, and build. Visual checks remain with the user.

#### Prefill with Tune

The product form can fill itself in from the manufacturer’s product page, for the owner to review field by field. It runs in the Products tab without the chat or an agent turn.

- **Link check:** half a second after a valid `https://` address is typed into **Manufacturer page**, the form asks `POST /api/admin/product-page/check`, which sends a HEAD request (then a GET whose body is never read, for servers that refuse HEAD) and reports `ok`, `blocked` (the page answered but refused the check, as bot walls and rate limits do), `missing` (404/410), or `unreachable`. Results are remembered per address while the form is open. A spinner shows inside the field while checking, and a green check when the page answered: an intentional reachable-status cue (the form’s only green). Other results show a short note beside the label (“Page not found”), and a polite live region announces each one.
- **Prefill:** **Prefill with Tune** (outlined, magic-wand icon) appears beside the field when the check is `ok`, or `blocked`, since Tavily may still read a page that refused a quick check. It hides after a successful prefill for that address and while any suggestions await review. Accepting suggestions keeps it hidden; **Reject all** allows a retry, and a different address can be prefilled once review is finished. The completed address is kept with the unsaved draft, including across closing or reloading the editor. When the button disappears, keyboard focus returns to the address if the button still had focus. It posts to `/api/admin/product-page/prefill`, which:
  - reads the page with [Tavily Extract](https://docs.tavily.com/documentation/api-reference/endpoint/extract) (`extract_depth: "advanced"` for script-rendered pages, tables, and galleries; Markdown; `include_images`; 30-second limit; an opaque, hashed `X-Human-Id` per admin). The whole page is read rather than `query`/`chunks_per_source` reranked chunks, because the model needs every photo in its place on the page; the text is capped at 40,000 characters instead;
  - reuses successful page reads through a server-only `"use cache"` function in [lib/admin/product-page-data.ts](lib/admin/product-page-data.ts), keyed by the normalized address (fragment removed, query preserved) and the admin's opaque ID. Reads refresh on the first request after 24 hours; after another minute, requests wait for the fresh read. Safe failure summaries last only one second so retries remain useful. Entries live in each server instance's memory and are lost on restart or deployment; separate instances can each read the page once. Every request still checks admin/origin access and drafts against current taxonomy and photo capacity, with fresh staged uploads; neither model drafts nor uploaded photo paths are reused. A closed editor skips drafting when a shared page read finishes;
  - folds each Markdown image into a numbered marker where it sits (“[image 3: Orbit Theory in walnut]”), adds Tavily’s other images, drops links, SVGs, GIFs, icons, and duplicate sizes of one photo (keeping the original), and offers up to 60 numbered images;
  - has the fast Haiku model (as for the card line) draft the form as structured output (`generateText` with `Output.object` and `extractJsonMiddleware`, [lib/admin/product-prefill.ts](lib/admin/product-prefill.ts)): the name (brand, then model), a brand and category from the shop’s own lists, the US list price (as MSRP) and each/pair, the description and card line under the copywriter’s rules, and 1–5 photos chosen from the numbered images by their descriptions, file names, and places in the text. The page is treated as data, not instructions, and the model never sees the images themselves;
  - keeps only what the form accepts (known slugs, a positive price, the length limits, offered image numbers within the product’s 20-photo limit), then downloads the chosen photos and stages them like the form’s own uploads: upright, at most 1,600px, WebP, metadata stripped, in `media/uploads/` as the admin (RLS). Photos under 500px on the long side are skipped as thumbnails.
- **Review:** each field Tune filled (name, category, brand, list price, each/pair, description, card line) gets a dashed edge with **Accept** (✓) and **Reject** (✕) inside it; suggested photos get the dashed edge and an Accept button beside Remove (Reject). Reject restores the value from before Prefill (an address still following the name follows it back); editing a marked field accepts it. The footer counts what’s left (“3 to review”) with **Accept all** and **Reject all**, which also deletes the rejected staged photos. Focus moves to the field (or to the manufacturer address when Prefill is hidden) when its buttons go. Fields the owner changed while Tune was reading are left alone, and a closed form or a changed address cancels the request (the server deletes photos it staged for it). Save and **Let Tune write it** wait while Prefill runs. The marks are monochrome, and the spinner holds still under reduced motion.
- **Safety:** both routes check the signed-in admin and that the request comes from this site, and return only short, safe messages; Tavily’s and the model’s replies never reach the browser. `TAVILY_API_KEY` stays on the server. Every address the server itself requests (the page check and photo downloads, in [lib/admin/public-fetch.ts](lib/admin/public-fetch.ts)) must be `https` on the default port, without credentials, to a public host; each name’s resolved addresses are checked as the connection opens and after every redirect, so private, loopback, link-local, and cloud-metadata addresses are refused even through DNS changes. Bodies are capped (20 MB per photo), and the whole prefill stops after 100 seconds.
- **Implementation:** [lib/admin/product-page.ts](lib/admin/product-page.ts) holds the check, the Tavily call, the page folding, prompt, draft checks, photo staging, and both routes’ logic, with dependencies passed in so `pnpm test:prefill` ([scripts/product-prefill.test.mjs](scripts/product-prefill.test.mjs)) runs it against fakes. eve’s [Tavily connection](https://eve.dev/integrations/tavily) isn’t used: it’s an MCP connection that signs each user in to their own Tavily account through Vercel Connect, for the agent, while the Products tab works without the agent and uses the site’s own key.
- **Limitations:** quality depends on the page: a page whose price isn’t in US dollars leaves MSRP empty, and one that blocks Tavily too fails with “Couldn’t read this page.” Photos are chosen from text and file names only, so a page with unlabeled, hash-named images may get weaker picks. A fresh page read spends Tavily credits (advanced extraction: 2 credits per 5 pages); cached reads avoid that extraction cost, while every Prefill still makes one Haiku call. On a test run with the U-Turn Orbit Theory page, the model step cost about $0.002 and took about 13 seconds. The `uploads/` copies of accepted photos stay behind after Save, like the form’s own uploads.

## Caching

Next’s Cache Components model is on (`cacheComponents: true` in [next.config.ts](next.config.ts)), with Partial Prefetching (`partialPrefetching: true`), which Next 16 recommends for new adopters and makes the default in its next major version.
- **Data:** each Supabase read in `lib/*-data.ts` is a `"use cache"` function that calls `cacheLife("content")` and `cacheTag(<tag>)` (`store-hours`, `news`, `deals`, `catalog`). `content` is a custom profile in `next.config.ts`: 5 minutes stale and revalidate, and no expiry, so a paused or unreachable Supabase project leaves the last good copy in place. Arguments become part of the cache key. `use cache` keeps entries in each server instance’s memory (the previous `unstable_cache` persisted them), but the prerendered pages themselves are stored and refreshed in the background.
- **Pages:** there’s no route segment config. Pages are prerendered static shells, and anything that depends on the URL (`params`, `searchParams`, `usePathname()`) reads it inside `<Suspense>`, so links prefetch each route’s shared App Shell. Everything else is in the shell. The build table marks fully static pages ○ and pages with streamed parts ◐.
- **Preserved pages:** Cache Components keeps up to 3 visited pages mounted but hidden (React `<Activity>`, `display: none`). Their effects clean up when hidden and run again when shown, while state and DOM stay. On the home page, the GSAP story’s pin is removed while hidden and rebuilt on return, and Back lands at the same scroll position. Two components were adjusted for this: LiquidGlass drops its SVG filter with the revoked displacement-map `blob:` URL when hidden, and the FAQ’s album grid ignores the 0×0 size a hidden page reports. New client code should clean up in effects, avoid document-wide queries that could match a hidden page’s elements, and ignore zero-size measurements.
- **Checking:** Next validates routes in development. Its insights appear in the dev overlay, in the browser console, and from the dev server’s MCP endpoint (`POST /_next/mcp`, tool `get_errors`). Load the routes you changed and check them before building.

## Supabase

Supabase holds the site’s editable content, images, and admin sign-in. News, QDeal deals, the shop catalog, and the store hours on the home page are served from it; the admin edits the hours, news, and deals through its assistant, and the catalog’s products in its Products tab.

- **Project:** `qaudio` (ref `kemumhpxapfadqsytudt`), region `us-east-1` (N. Virginia), Postgres 17. It’s on the Free plan in the Vercel-linked organization “gaiborjosue’s projects”. Free projects pause after a stretch of inactivity, so plan a paid tier before launch.
- **Migrations:** SQL files in [supabase/migrations](supabase/migrations), named `<applied version>_<name>.sql`. They were applied through the Supabase MCP server.
  - `harden_default_privileges`: new tables, sequences, and functions in `public` get no automatic grants for `anon`, `authenticated`, or `service_role`, and functions are no longer executable by everyone. Every exposed object needs an explicit grant plus RLS.
  - `admins_and_news_posts`: an internal `private` schema (not exposed through the Data API) with `private.admins` (one row per admin user) and `private.is_admin()`, which RLS policies call. Admin rights change on the next request, with no stale JWT claims. `public.news_posts` holds slug (the live permalinks), title, excerpt, `body` (a JSON array of the `NewsBlock` blocks in `lib/news.ts`), cover path, size, alt text and fit, `status` (`draft`/`published`), `published_at`, and timestamps. `updated_at` is maintained by a trigger. A partial index on `(published_at desc, id desc)` serves the feed and keyset paging. A published post needs a date and a cover.
  - `media_bucket`: a public `media` storage bucket (images only: WebP, JPEG, PNG, AVIF; 10 MB each), organized by folder such as `news/…`. Anyone can load a file by its public URL; only admins can list, upload, replace, or delete. `next.config.ts` allows `next/image` to optimize files from it.
  - `news_feed_function`: `public.news_feed(after_published_at, after_id, max_rows)` returns published cards (no bodies) after a `(published_at, id)` cursor, newest first, capped at 50 rows. It runs as the caller, so RLS still applies; `anon`, `authenticated`, and `service_role` may execute it.
  - `deals`: `public.deals` (title, summary, note, `status`, `sort_order`, optional `starts_at`/`ends_at`, timestamps) and `public.deal_items` (its products: `deal_id`, brand, name, optional `condition` and `price_cents`, `sort_order`, timestamps), both with the `updated_at` trigger. Products are deleted with their deal, and an index on `(deal_id, sort_order)` serves the foreign key and product order.
  - `store_hours`: `public.store_weekly_hours` has exactly seven rows, one per weekday (`weekday` 0 = Monday). Each holds `kind` (`open`/`appointment`/`closed`) and `opens_at`/`closes_at` times; nobody can insert or delete rows, only edit them. `public.store_hours_changes` holds dated overrides: `starts_on` and an inclusive `ends_on`, `kind` and times, an optional `note`, and timestamps. Checks require times exactly when `kind` is `open` (opening before closing) and `ends_on` on or after `starts_on`. Both tables have the `updated_at` trigger, and an index on `ends_on` serves the read of changes that haven’t ended. The migration also moved in the hours that used to live in code: the regular week, Nov 6 1–3 pm, and closed Nov 7–15, 2026.
  - `shop_catalog`: `public.catalog_categories` and `public.catalog_brands` (slug primary keys, name, artwork or logo path under `/categories/` or `/brands/` in `public/`, `sort_order`), seeded with the 14 categories and 23 brands that used to live in code. `public.products` holds the fields listed in [Shop](#shop), with checks on slugs, lengths, positive prices, `price_unit`, `condition`, `status`, and an `https://` manufacturer URL; `currency` is fixed to `USD`. It has the `updated_at` trigger and indexes on `(category_slug, sort_order, id)` and `(brand_slug, sort_order, id)`. `public.product_images` (`product_id`, `path` under `products/`, alt, width, height, `sort_order`, unique per product) is deleted with its product.
  - `catalog_category_wheel_fields`: adds the home wheel’s `wheel_label` (1–40 characters) and `scene_path` (under `/category-scenes/`) to `catalog_categories`, filled from the old `lib/categories.ts`, which was then removed with `lib/brands.ts`, so the database is the only source. The existing table grants and RLS cover the new columns.
  - `public_is_admin_function`: `public.is_admin()`, a security-invoker wrapper around `private.is_admin()` that only `authenticated` may execute, so the admin sign-in and the admin agent can ask the Data API whether the signed-in user is an admin. It was checked as `anon` (refused), a signed-in non-admin (`false`), and the admin (`true`).
- **Access rules:**
  - Visitors read published posts whose `published_at` has passed (a future time schedules a post).
  - Visitors read published deals inside their optional `starts_at`/`ends_at` window, and only those deals’ products.
  - Visitors read all store hours and hour changes.
  - Visitors read all categories and brands, published products, and the images of products they can see.
  - Signed-in non-admins get the same view.
  - Admins read every post and deal and can add, edit, and delete them and their products. They can edit the weekly hours and add, edit, and delete hour changes, and they can read draft products and add, edit, and delete categories, brands, products, and product images.
  - Server code using the secret key has explicit grants.

  These were checked by running queries as `anon`, as a signed-in non-admin, and as an admin inside a rolled-back transaction. After the import, the publishable key read the feed in two pages but was refused when updating a post or uploading a file. For deals, visitors and non-admins saw only live deals (not drafts, future or expired ones) and their products, and non-admin writes were refused or changed no rows. For the catalog, run [scripts/verify-catalog-access.sql](scripts/verify-catalog-access.sql) as the database owner (SQL editor or MCP) after any catalog schema or policy change. In a rolled-back transaction, it first counts the live tables, then adds a hidden draft product and checks each role. Visitors and signed-in non-admins must read every category and brand but only published products and their images, and must be refused (or match no rows) on every insert, update, and delete. Admins and the secret key must read everything, including drafts, and write their own probe rows. Because the counts come from the data, it needs no edits as products are added. It returns “Catalog access checks passed”, or raises one error naming each failed check. The only performance advisory is an informational “unused index” notice for the new `deal_items` index. The only security advisory is leaked-password protection (an Auth setting), which matters only if admins sign in with passwords.

- **Seed and import:** [supabase/seed/news.json](supabase/seed/news.json) holds the migrated posts in table form, and [supabase/seed/news](supabase/seed/news) holds their 20 photos. Preserve uncommitted work, minimal copy, accessible headings, keyboard/focus behavior, reduced motion, and established animation behavior.

Keep this README current when features, architecture/routes, setup/dependencies, asset sources, or limitations change. Update AGENTS when durable workflow conventions change. Documentation is part of task completion.

Use proportionate checks: `pnpm lint` and `pnpm typecheck` for code changes (both pass with no errors and should stay that way), and a production build when routing or build behavior is affected. Visual verification is user-led; do not use the computer-use skill to verify changes. For interaction changes, the relevant checks include desktop/mobile layouts, forward/reverse scrolling, return anchors, wheel inputs/sound, category/brand destinations, one-click brand navigation, modal behavior, news feed paging and its back/reload position, keyboard focus, and reduced motion. ### Next.js practices

Audited against the installed Next 16.4 docs (production checklist, caching, error handling, data security). In place:
- **Rendering:** pages are Server Components; `"use client"` sits only on interactive pieces. Cache Components and Partial Prefetching are on: pages prerender static shells, and anything that depends on the URL streams inside `<Suspense>` (see [Caching](#caching)).
- **Data:** Server Components read Supabase directly through `server-only` modules in `lib/*-data.ts`, as tagged `"use cache"` functions fetched in parallel. `/api/news` serves the client-side feed and validates its query (400 on a bad limit or cursor); `/auth/confirm` only turns Supabase email links into a session. The secret key is used only by the import scripts. Admin pages read the session inside `<Suspense>` through `sessionSupabase()`, which awaits `connection()` first: Supabase compares the token’s expiry with the clock, so those reads run only at request time, never while Next prerenders or prefetches the App Shell.
- **Routing:** each product has its own URL and page. The shop shows it as a modal through a parallel `@modal` slot and an intercepting route (see [Shop](#shop)).
- **Images and fonts:** `next/image` everywhere except vendored components and the WebGL loader. Above-the-fold images use `loading="eager"` (not the deprecated `priority`). Fonts come from `next/font`, and the unused Bodoni isn’t preloaded.
- **Metadata:** the root layout’s title template (`%s — Q Audio`) and a description; pages set their own titles. Product pages add a description, an Open Graph image, and schema.org `Product` JSON-LD.
- **Errors:** custom 404, error, and global-error pages (see [Experience and routes](#experience-and-routes)).
- **Security:** `next.config.ts` sends a `Content-Security-Policy` and these headers on every response:
  - `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`.
  - `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin`.
  - `X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN`.
  - A `Permissions-Policy` that turns off camera, geolocation, and topics. Microphone is allowed for this origin on `/admin` pages (permission is still requested by the browser); it is disabled elsewhere.

  It also drops `X-Powered-By`.

  The CSP has no nonces, because a per-request nonce would force every prerendered page to render on demand (and doesn’t work with Partial Prerendering). So scripts allow `'unsafe-inline'` for Next’s inline scripts (plus `'unsafe-eval'` in development only), but load only from this origin.

  Everything else is restricted: `default-src 'self'`, images from this origin, `blob:`, `data:`, and the Supabase origin, and `connect-src` to this origin and Supabase. It also sets `object-src 'none'`, `base-uri 'self'`, `form-action 'self'`, and `frame-ancestors 'self'`.

  Pages were checked with no violations in development and in a production build. Add any new external origin (analytics, embeds) to it. `upgrade-insecure-requests` is left out because it breaks `next start` over plain HTTP on localhost; HTTPS comes from the host.
- **Lint:** `pnpm lint` has no errors.

Not yet done, and why:
- **A strict, nonce-based CSP:** it suits the admin area, whose pages render per request anyway. Set it in `proxy.ts` for `/admin` only, and exclude `/admin` from the `next.config.ts` CSP so the two policies don’t combine.
- **`metadataBase`, sitemap, robots, and default Open Graph images:** they wait for the production domain.
- **Soft 404s for unknown slugs:** they return 200 with `noindex` (see [Experience and routes](#experience-and-routes)), the documented cost of streaming URL-dependent content.
- **Brand wall links:** the tiles are plain `<a>` links, so each click is a full page load that replays the loader.
- **Admin work:** follow the [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) conventions. Admin Server Actions re-check the signed-in admin in every action, with Proxy only for an optimistic redirect. They validate input on the server, write with the user’s session so RLS applies, and call `updateTag(<tag>)` afterward; the single-argument `revalidateTag(tag)` is deprecated in Next 16. The admin assistant’s writes are the documented exception: approved tool calls write as the admin from the agent’s process, and the chat refreshes the tags afterward (see [Admin assistant](#admin-assistant)). The Products tab follows the rule: its saves and deletes are Server Actions.

Store hours, schedule changes, news posts, and QDeal deals are edited through the admin assistant at `/admin`, and products in its Products tab (`/admin/products`); shop categories and brands are edited in Metadata (`/admin/metadata`). If Supabase is unreachable or the Free-plan project is paused, `/`, `/news`, article pages, `/qdeal` and the shop pages keep serving their last generated version, but loading more news cards fails to the “Load more” button and `pnpm build` fails. News posts’ product links still point to `https://www.qaudio.com/products/…` pages on the current site. This prototype now serves the same `/products/<slug>` paths, so once the domain moves, each link works when a product with that slug is published in the catalog; until then those links show “Page not found”. QDeal’s client-side “visit us” link lands on the home page’s visit section, but the pinned story can flash for a few frames on the way while its layout sets up. When a back, forward, or reload restores the feed far down, a lazily loaded card can become the LCP image, and Next’s dev LCP warning names it. Live hours status depends on the visitor’s device clock. Browser audio policies can suppress the tick, and glass rendering varies across browsers. Source inspection alone does not certify cross-browser visual behavior. The brand wall’s search matches brand names only (not products or categories), and on a phone a search with more than 9 results leaves the wall drifting rather than holding still.
